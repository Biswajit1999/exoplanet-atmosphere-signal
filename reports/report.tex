\begin{abstract}
We reproduce the carbon-monoxide sub-band statistic reported for the
JWST/NIRSpec G395H transmission spectrum of WASP-39 b using the complete
three-file public data release of Grant et al. (2023).  The released arrays
give an in-band minus out-of-band mean transit-depth contrast of
\SI{263.66 +- 67.68}{ppm}, Welch $t=3.895$, and one-sided
$p=6.30\times10^{-5}$.  A deterministic 10,000-resample bootstrap yields a
95\% interval of \SIrange{130.38}{394.03}{ppm}.  Both broad wavelength zones
retain positive contrasts, and deleting any single contiguous selected run
changes the estimate only to \SIrange{244.7}{280.3}{ppm}.  These calculations
reproduce a model-informed test on released derived spectra; they are not an
independent detector-level reduction, blind molecule search, or atmospheric
abundance retrieval.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scientific question}
The 4.6-$\mu$m fundamental band of CO is resolved into structured opacity at
the native resolution of NIRSpec G395H.  Grant et al. constructed ``in'' and
``out'' wavelength selections using continuum-removed CO-only and non-CO
models, then tested whether observed transit depths in the CO-selected bands
were greater.  Our target estimand is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = \overline{d}_{\mathrm{in}}-\overline{d}_{\mathrm{out}},
\end{equation}
where $d$ is transit depth in parts per million.  This fixed estimand avoids
recasting the released model curves as if they had been refit in this work.

\section{Data and provenance}
All three files in Zenodo record 7866690 are versioned with the analysis:
the spectroscopic light curves, the native and binned transmission spectra
with model curves, and the exact sub-band samples.  The manifest records the
source URL, retrieval time, byte size, and SHA-256 for each product.  The
primary computation uses 111 in-band and 145 out-of-band values.  The article
text reports 148 out-of-band values; we preserve the archived arrays without
adding or removing samples and disclose this release-level discrepancy.

\section{Statistical methods}
For independent group means with unequal variances, the standard error is
\begin{equation}
  s_{\Delta}=\sqrt{\frac{s_{\mathrm{in}}^2}{n_{\mathrm{in}}}
  +\frac{s_{\mathrm{out}}^2}{n_{\mathrm{out}}}},
\end{equation}
and the test statistic is $t=\Delta/s_{\Delta}$ with Welch--Satterthwaite
degrees of freedom.  The directional alternative, specified by the physical
band definition, is $\Delta>0$.  We additionally resample within each group
10,000 times, perform a 20,000-draw label permutation as an exploratory null
check, split the selected points into 4.4--4.7 and 4.7--5.0 $\mu$m zones, and
delete contiguous wavelength runs one at a time to expose local leverage.

The archive also supplies full and no-CO atmospheric curves.  We calculate
their weighted $\chi^2$ against the spectrum as a descriptive residual
diagnostic.  Because neither curve is refit and the release does not encode
the likelihood construction and fitted parameter counts, we do not calculate
AIC, BIC, Bayes factors, abundance posteriors, or a detection significance
from their difference.

The reproduced point estimate and Welch uncertainty agree with the article's
quoted \SI{264 +- 68}{ppm}.  Positive contrasts in both broad wavelength
zones show that the result is not supplied by only one half of the CO region.
The narrow leave-one-run range indicates that no single contiguous selected
run dominates the overall mean difference.

For the 1008-point native spectrum, the fixed full curve has
$\chi^2/N=1.145$ and improves $\chi^2$ by 75.65 relative to the fixed no-CO
curve.  This is useful as a residual diagnostic but does not change the
inferential boundary above.

\section{Limitations}
The primary bands were selected using atmospheric models and are therefore
not a blind search.  The input is a released derived spectrum rather than raw
detector ramps.  Nearby native-resolution pixels may retain correlation, so
the nominal Welch probability is conditional on independence and the stated
band-selection procedure.  The bootstrap likewise resamples individual
selected depths; the wavelength-zone and deletion diagnostics partly expose,
but do not fully model, correlation.  A stronger independent analysis would
begin with uncalibrated exposures, propagate detector systematics, freeze the
band-selection rule before inspecting the target, and repeat the experiment
across visits or instruments.

\section{Reproducibility}
The repository includes pinned Python dependencies, deterministic random
seeds, unit and integration tests, machine-readable results, figure sidecars,
source checksums, and continuous integration on supported Python versions.
The complete workflow is run with
\texttt{python scripts/run\_analysis.py} followed by
\texttt{python scripts/make\_figures.py}.
